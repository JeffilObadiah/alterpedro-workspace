## 1. Core company concept

**ALTER PEDRO is a “Solution Company.”**

It should **not** be positioned primarily as:

- a software agency
- web-development agency
- consultancy
- AI agency
- analytics company
- design studio
- generalist IT services company
- traditional product studio

The central internal truth is:

> **We turn ambiguous problems into working solutions.**

The strongest public promise developed:

> **Bring us the problem. We’ll figure out the rest.**

Brand/category structure:

> **ALTER PEDRO**  
> **THE SOLUTION COMPANY.**

The breadth of capabilities is **not the product**.

The product is:

> **ALTER PEDRO's ability to understand a problem, figure out what actually needs to happen, and execute the solution.**

The customer should **not need to know the technical answer beforehand**.

They shouldn't have to decide:

> “Do I need an app, AI, automation, analytics, a website, an ERP, an integration, consulting, or something else?”

They explain the situation/outcome.

**ALTER PEDRO figures out the solution.**

# 7. New core idea: AI Problem Discovery / Diagnostic Engine

Instead of immediately charging someone to diagnose their problem:

> **Give them the diagnostic experience first.**

The diagnostic engine itself becomes the **first ALTER PEDRO product experience**.

It should NOT feel like:

- a boring form
- a lead-generation questionnaire
- 20 fields
- a service-selection form
- a generic chatbot
- “AI magic” that spits out nonsense

The experience should be:

> **Tell us what's going on. We'll help you figure it out.**

The user explains their situation naturally.

The AI listens, understands, reflects, identifies missing information, and asks the **next best question**.

---

# 8. The “make the user feel heard” requirement

This is a critical design goal.

The engine isn't merely supposed to **extract lead information**.

The user should feel:

> **“They actually understood me.”**

After an answer, the AI should sometimes reflect the user's meaning before asking the next question.

Example:

User:

> “Our sales team wastes hours every week making reports.”

PEDRO:

> **“Got it. So the issue isn't really the reports themselves — it's that your sales team is spending hours manually assembling information every week.”**

Then:

> **“Let me dig into that.”**

Then asks the next relevant question.

This creates the psychological experience of:

> **“Oh shit, it actually understood what I meant.”**

That feeling is central to the product.

# 9. Seven questions — but NOT a fixed questionnaire

The user wants roughly **7 questions**.

Important interpretation:

> **7 dynamic conversational turns, NOT seven predetermined questions.**

The engine should not literally execute:

```text
Question 1
Question 2
Question 3
Question 4
Question 5
Question 6
Question 7
```

Instead, AI decides what to ask based on the current problem model.

The question should answer:

> **“What is the most valuable thing we still don't know?”**

Example:

### Q1
> **What's happening?**

User:

> “Our sales team wastes hours every week putting reports together.”

AI extracts:

- reporting problem
- manual work
- sales operations
- time cost

### Q2

Instead of an irrelevant generic question:

> **“What does that reporting process look like today?”**

User:

> “They export data from three systems, clean it in Excel, then someone manually makes a report.”

AI now understands:

- multiple systems
- fragmented data
- Excel dependency
- manual transformation
- reporting workflow

### Q3

> **“How often does this happen, and roughly how much time does it take?”**

Possible later questions:

> **“Who else is affected by this?”**

> **“What happens if nothing changes?”**

> **“Have you already tried fixing it?”**

> **“If this were solved tomorrow, what would be different?”**

But these are **examples**, not a fixed script.

The AI decides the sequence dynamically.

The AI should constantly determine:

Not:

> What question comes next in my form?

# What ChatGPT's summary missed

The summary is strong. It captures the positioning, philosophy, methodology, website structure, capability universe, and the critical shift away from paid diagnostics. But it **missed or under-captured several specific mechanics** that came out of the last exchange — the ones that make the diagnostic engine actually work in practice.

Here's what's missing:

---

## 1. The two-layer commercial model (free scan + paid Sprint)

The summary says "don't charge someone just for telling you their problem" and mentions a paid Blueprint "after trust has been established." But it doesn't capture the **specific two-layer structure** that solved the scam problem:

- **Layer 1 — The Scan.** Free, instant, AI-driven. 3 minutes. Real value back. No gate.
- **Layer 2 — The Solution Sprint.** Paid, human-led, half-day. **Credited against the project if they proceed.**

That credit-back mechanic is the single line that kills the scam feeling. The summary doesn't have it.

---

## 2. The "Solution Sprint" framing

The summary still calls the paid layer a "Blueprint" (Section 22). But the last exchange proposed renaming the paid engagement to **Solution Sprint** or **Blueprint Session** — because "diagnostic" and "audit" sound like you're charging to find the problem. A Sprint sounds like you're charging to *design the solution with them*. That distinction matters.

---

## 3. The specific 7-question coverage map

The summary correctly says "7 dynamic conversational turns, NOT seven predetermined questions" and gives examples. But it **doesn't preserve the specific 7-area framework** that was proposed:

1. **The Situation** — What's actually happening?
2. **The Cost** — What is this costing you?
3. **The History** — What have you already tried?
4. **The Constraint** — What's really standing in the way?
5. **The Vision** — What does "solved" look like?
6. **The Stake** — Who else is affected, who decides?
7. **The Urgency** — When does this need to move?

These are the *coverage map*, not the script. The AI hits all 7 without ever reading them off a list. That distinction is in the last exchange but not in the summary.

---

## 4. The dynamic questioning mechanics

The summary says the AI decides the next question dynamically. But it doesn't capture the **specific mechanics**:

- **Reorder on the fly** — if they open with "we tried X and it failed," jump to History first.
- **Follow up when it matters** — "roughly how many deals, and what's a deal worth?"
- **Compress when clear** — if one answer covers 3 areas, mark them answered and move on.
- **Expand when vague** — "give me one example from last week where this got in the way."
- **Hard cap at ~7–10 exchanges** — after that it feels like work. Summarize and deliver.

---

## 5. The specific summary output structure (the "payoff moment")

The summary has an example Problem Brief (Section 13), but it doesn't capture the **specific 5-part structure** proposed for the end-of-scan summary:

1. **What we heard** — 2–3 sentences, their words mirrored back.
2. **What seems to be the real problem** — stated vs. underlying. The insight moment.
3. **What we'd want to investigate next** — 3–4 directions.
4. **What we'd need to know more about** — honest gaps.
5. **Suggested next step** — two doors: 20-min call (free) or Solution Sprint (paid, credited).

The summary's Problem Brief is good but more clinical. This structure is warmer and more conversion-oriented.

## 6. The "feeling heard" UX mechanics

Section 8 covers the *principle* of feeling heard. But the last exchange proposed **specific UI/UX mechanics** that aren't captured:

- **Echo before you ask** — after every answer, reflect it back in one line before the next question. This does 80% of the "feeling heard" work.
- **Never ask what they already told you** — the engine holds context.
- **Open text and voice input, no forced dropdowns** — dropdowns feel like a survey.
- **Live understanding panel** — side panel showing "We're building a picture of: your workflow · your cost · your constraints."
- **Allow "I don't know"** — a non-punishing button. Critical for people who don't know their own bottleneck.
- **End with a summary that sounds like a person wrote it** — not bullet points.
